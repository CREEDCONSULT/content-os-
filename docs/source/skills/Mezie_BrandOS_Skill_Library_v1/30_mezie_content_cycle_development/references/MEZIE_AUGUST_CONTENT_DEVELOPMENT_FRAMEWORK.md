# 3. CONTENT GOVERNANCE PRINCIPLES

Every item must comply with the following rules.

## 3.1 Truth Before Performance

No content may contain:

- Fabricated personal experiences
- Invented results
- False client outcomes
- Unsupported market claims
- Fake urgency
- False quotes
- Made-up analytics
- Unverified statistics
- Claims of authority beyond the documented authority ladder

The Founder Identity OS explicitly requires truth, evidence, honesty about limitations, and a distinction between demonstrated, developing, and exploratory authority.

## 3.2 Evidence Before Opinion

Every content item must be classified as one of the following:

### Evidence-Based Explanation

Supported by research, documents, demonstrations, project records, platform data, credible reports, or direct experience.

### Founder Observation

A clearly framed personal interpretation derived from lived experience.

### Working Hypothesis

An argument being explored, not presented as settled fact.

### Personal Reflection

A subjective interpretation of an event, belief, decision, or experience.

### Demonstration

Proof through screen recording, workflow footage, project documentation, implementation, comparison, or result.

### Commentary

A reaction to a current development, with fact and opinion explicitly separated.

No hypothesis may be written as a fact.

No isolated observation may be presented as a trend.

BrandOS already establishes that a single-source observation cannot be treated as an established trend and that facts, strategy, inference, and hypotheses must remain separated.

## 3.3 Originality Before Imitation

Creator references may inform:

- Hook type
- Pacing
- Scene structure
- Camera logic
- Format
- Narrative mechanism
- Information sequencing
- Caption structure
- Editing rhythm
- CTA mechanics

They must not provide:

- Copied wording
- Copied stories
- Copied persona
- Copied worldview
- Copied distinctive visual identity
- Near-identical scripts
- A manufactured personality

Instagram has continued emphasizing original content in its recommendation and creator guidance, including protections intended to favour original creators over republished material.

## 3.4 Sustainable Quality Before Unsustainable Volume

Content volume must be determined by:

- Available research time
- On-camera availability
- Editing capacity
- Access to locations
- B-roll availability
- Timeliness
- Required evidence
- Production complexity
- Repurposing potential

The system must warn against any calendar whose production burden exceeds the available capacity. This is already a BrandOS editorial-calendar requirement.

## 3.5 Platform-Native Adaptation

One idea can become multiple assets, but each platform output must be re-authored for that platform.

Repurposing must not mean:

> Upload the exact same file with the same caption everywhere.

It must mean:

> Preserve the intellectual core while modifying format, duration, opening, visual rhythm, caption, CTA, and context.

# 8. PRODUCTION FORMAT LIBRARY

## 8.1 Direct Talking Head

### Best Used When

- The idea depends on credibility
- The message is personal
- The argument needs emotional conviction
- The explanation is simple enough to deliver without heavy supporting visuals
- The audience needs to see Mezie

### Weak Use Cases

- Complex workflows
- Interface demonstrations
- Detailed market charts
- Multi-stage visual explanations

### Standard Capture

- 9:16 master
- Medium close-up
- Eye-level camera
- Clean depth between subject and background
- Controlled sound
- Captions
- Alternative close-up crop where resolution allows
- Five seconds of silence and neutral expression at beginning and end for editing

---

## 8.2 Guided Talking Head

Mezie speaks to camera while visual evidence appears beside, behind, or over him.

### Best Used When

- Explaining diagrams
- Presenting evidence
- Breaking down documents
- Commenting on tools
- Walking through projects

### Required Assets

- Screenshots
- Charts
- Documents
- Headlines
- Interface captures
- Diagrams
- B-roll
- Keyword graphics

---

## 8.3 Screen-Recorded Explanation

### Best Used When

- The interface is the evidence
- The process is more important than performance
- A system must be demonstrated
- The audience needs procedural clarity

### Required Capture

- Clean browser
- Hidden private tabs
- Enlarged cursor
- Controlled zoom
- No visible confidential information
- High-resolution recording
- Separate microphone recording where possible
- Predefined cursor route

---

## 8.4 Voice-Over Documentary

### Best Used When

- The story is visual
- The founder is working
- The content needs atmosphere
- The piece documents a process
- Direct delivery would feel forced

### Required Structure

- Story spine before filming
- B-roll mapped to every sentence
- Ambient sound opportunities
- Establishing shot
- Process shots
- Human detail
- Friction
- Outcome or unresolved next step

---

## 8.5 Builder Walk

A moving talking-head format in which Mezie explains an idea while walking through an environment relevant to the message.

### Best Used When

- The subject is conceptual

- Business district

- Park or urban walkway

- Transit between work locations

Movement must support the idea rather than distract from it.

# 15. HOOK DEVELOPMENT FRAMEWORK

Each content item should generate at least five hook candidates across distinct mechanisms.

## 15.1 Tension Hook

## 15.4 Proof Hook

> “I turned this business idea into a complete operating system before writing a line of production code.”

## 15.5 Question Hook

> “What exactly happens between having an idea and owning a working business?”

## 15.6 Story Hook

> “My first business did not fail because people stopped buying.”

## 15.7 Consequence Hook

> “The founders who cannot explain their own process will struggle to use AI well.”

## 15.8 Visual Hook

Begin with:

- A finished dashboard
- A workflow animation
- A dramatic before-and-after
- A physical document
- A surprising screen result
- A strong location
- A moment of action before explanation

## 15.9 Hook Selection Rule

The selected hook must:

- Accurately represent the content
- Create curiosity without deception
- Be understandable without prior context
- Speak to the intended audience
- Lead naturally into the first supporting point
- Work visually and verbally

YouTube’s official guidance continues to emphasize that titles and thumbnails should accurately represent content rather than mislead viewers.

# 16. SCRIPTING SYSTEM

## 16.1 Short-Form Script Architecture

### 0–3 seconds: Interruption

- Claim
- Question
- Contrast
- Visual proof
- Tension

### 3–10 seconds: Relevance

Explain why the viewer should care.

### 10–35 seconds: Development

Deliver the core insight through:

- Three points
- A sequence
- A comparison
- A demonstration
- A mini-story
- A problem-and-solution structure

### 35–50 seconds: Resolution

State the implication.

### 50–60 seconds: Action

CTA, question, save prompt, follow prompt, or transition to a deeper asset.

This timing is a working production template, not a universal platform law.

LinkedIn’s own video guidance emphasizes that early seconds are critical, recommends opening with substance rather than branding, and encourages mobile-first, captioned video.

## 20.3 Repurposing Rules
